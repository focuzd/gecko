    How we measured

    The benchmark

    • 6 openings in 3 groups: familiar ("Once upon a time"), altered ("Once upon a time, a robot named Zed"), and unfamiliar ("The scientist looked at the data and").
    • 4 stories per opening with the same seeds for every model, temperature 0.8, up to 600 new characters.
    • Controls: 12 real stories set 100 and 6 random-character strings set 0.
    • Automatic checks: real-word rate, repetition (distinct-2), ending on its own, longest copied span.

    The judge

    • One fresh judge per story (Claude Haiku 5.5), so it can't compare stories or drift.
    • Blind: the judge never knows which model wrote the story.
    • 7 criteria, scored 1 to 5 against a written rubric, with a justification first. Consistency, structure and meaning count 1.5×.
    • Paired tests: stories with the same opening and seed are compared head to head.

    Where things stand

    The encoder-decoder

    Built and tested at small scale. The encoder reads the older 256 characters and the decoder writes the newest 256. Three 11M-parameter versions (3+3, 2+4 and 4+2 layers) are ready to train.

    A 1M-parameter test reached validation loss 1.17 and wrote clean openings, but barely used its encoder: a full encoder improved its loss by only 0.005.

    Next steps

    • Word-piece tokens (BPE), the best bet for moving meaning past 2 out of 5.
    • Sampling settings (top-k, repetition penalty) on the best model, no training needed.
    • fp16 training as its own experiment, for faster runs.
    • Full encoder-decoder runs, then TinyStories-Instruct.
    • A second seed and a judge re-check, to measure noise.
